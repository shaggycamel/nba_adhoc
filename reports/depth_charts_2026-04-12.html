Depth charts from the team hierarchy model, for every team's next game as of 12 April 2026. Three charts are inline below plus two league-wide tables. All 30 teams (545 players) are committed to the repo on branch heir-mod as reports/depth_charts_2026-04-12.csv, with a plain-text companion beside it.

Why that date: the local Parquet snapshot ends with the 2025-26 season, and 12 April 2026 is its last regular-season day on which all 30 teams played. It is late season, so there is a lot of resting and injury in it — which makes it a good showcase but a heavier slate than a typical night.

Reading the columns

position / depthassigned position, and rank within it on this team

minutes_if_playsexpected minutes conditional on playing
expected_minutesp_play × minutes_if_plays — the headline number
expected_usageexpected share of team possessions used while on the floor

Biggest absorption gains

Available players who climb furthest among their available team-mates, with the minutes their team expects to have vacated. This is what the model is for: the backup who inherits a starter's role.

Read ranks gained together with same pos vacated, because the measure picks up two different things. A big gain alongside a big same-position vacancy is absorption proper — Nikola Topić with 100 guard minutes vacated at Oklahoma City. A big gain with almost nothing vacated usually means the player is himself returning from a spell out: his own trailing minutes have decayed, so he sits low on the full roster but first among those available. Stephen Curry (2.4 vacated) and Cade Cunningham (2.2) are that second case, not the first.

What to trust and what not to

  • Expected minutes are the solid output. R² 0.787, mean absolute error 4.0 minutes against what players actually played, on held-out seasons.
  • Expected usage is weak. It barely beats a trailing average (R² 0.342 against 0.331). Game-level usage is mostly noise once minutes are known — lean on the minutes column.
  • PG/SG and SF/PF are a convention, not a label. No source in the database carries the five positions; guard-versus-forward-versus-centre is learned and holds 97% game to game, but the split within guards and within forwards is imposed, holds 87%, and about a quarter of those calls rest on a margin thin enough to be arbitrary. Treat C and the coarse slot as reliable.
  • Blank rank_gain means the player has almost no chance of playing, so the figure carries no information — every unavailable player ties at zero expected minutes.
  • Team totals do not add up, and the gap is informative. Each player is predicted independently, so a healthy team lands near its ~236-minute budget (Orlando 243) while a heavily depleted one falls far short — Boston totals 136 here, Minnesota 151. The correlation between a team's total and its number of players out is −0.54. So on a depleted team, read the ordering and the rank gains rather than the absolute minutes: somebody has to play those missing minutes and the model spreads less than the full budget. Rescaling each team to the budget was tested and did not improve per-player accuracy, so it is deliberately left alone.
